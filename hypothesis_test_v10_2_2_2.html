How sampling connects all 3 tabs

  • 1. The Trial (p-value): Repeated sampling under H₀ (μ = 0) builds the null sampling distribution so we can measure how extreme our 1 observed sample (+0.38) is.
  • 2. The Risk (α): Repeated sampling under H₀ (μ = 0) shows what fraction of random samples cross the rejection cutoff by chance alone.
  • 3. The Catch (Power): Repeated sampling under H₁ (μ = d) shows what fraction of random samples cross that same cutoff when a real change exists.

Remember

  • A p-value assumes H₀ is true—it is not the probability that H₀ is true, and neither decision proves a hypothesis.
  • The lab uses a one-sample z-test. It simulates independent, normally distributed changes with a known standard deviation of 1 point. Changing n or effect size starts a fresh run; on Tab 2, changing α reclassifies the same batch.

    Symbols and abbreviations

    Sample and test: H0 = null hypothesis; H1 = alternative hypothesis; n = observations per experiment; x̄ = sample mean; μ = population mean; μ₀ = mean specified by H0; SD = standard deviation; σ = population standard deviation; SE = standard error of the sample mean; z = distance from the null mean in standard-error units; p = p-value calculated under H0.

    Errors and detection: α (alpha) = planned false-alarm probability; β (beta) = probability of missing the specified effect; power = probability of detecting that effect; d = population mean difference from zero divided by population standard deviation; δ (delta) = d√n, the shift in the z distribution; c = planned z cutoff.

    Probability and notation: Φ (phi) = standard normal cumulative distribution function, giving area to the left of a value; SF = survival function, giving area to its right: SF(a) = 1 − Φ(a); Φ⁻¹ = inverse cumulative distribution function, giving the z value for a cumulative probability; P(A | B) = probability of A assuming B; |z| = absolute value of z; √ = square root; Σ = sum; ≈ = approximately equal.

        Test ingredients and assumptions

        The central question

        How compatible is the observed result with the no-effect model and its assumptions?

        Null hypothesis (H0)

        No effect / status quo

        H0: μ = 0
        Alternative hypothesis (H1)

        A real effect exists

        H1: μ1 ≠ μ0

        The test statistic (z)

        The test statistic summarizes how far the observed result is from what H0 predicts.

        Z = (x̄ − μ0) / (σ / √n)

        More extreme Z in the direction(s) named by H1 → more evidence against H0.

        Choosing z, t, or F · further reference

        For this mean test, σ is known and we use z. A one-sample t test estimates the unknown population standard deviation (SD) from the sample and has n − 1 degrees of freedom; as degrees of freedom grow, its reference distribution approaches the standard normal. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) compares between-group and within-group variation using F. A significant omnibus F result indicates that at least one group mean differs; follow-up comparisons investigate where. All these tests require their own assumptions and a specified null model.
