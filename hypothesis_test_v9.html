    Optional refresher: the test ingredients

    The central question

    How compatible is the observed result with the no-effect model and its assumptions?

    Null hypothesis (H0)

    No effect / status quo

    H0: μ = 0
    Alternative hypothesis (H1)

    A real effect exists

    H1: μ1 ≠ μ0

    The test statistic (z)

    The test statistic summarizes how far the observed result is from what H0 predicts.

    Z = (x̄ − μ0) / (σ / √n)

    More extreme Z in the direction(s) named by H1 → more evidence against H0.

    Choosing z, t, or F · further reference

    For this mean test, σ is known and we use z. A one-sample t test estimates the unknown population SD from the sample and has n − 1 degrees of freedom; as degrees of freedom grow, its reference distribution approaches the standard normal. One-way ANOVA compares between-group and within-group variation using F. A significant omnibus F result indicates that at least one group mean differs; follow-up comparisons investigate where. All these tests require their own assumptions and a specified null model.
